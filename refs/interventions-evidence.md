# Interventions evidence
> Smartphone-overuse interventions with a measured effect, scored against "never blocks", plus an audit of the Insights claims.

Gathered 2026-11-06 for SPECS § Visual (Patterns row) and ROADMAP § App screens (Insights audit).
Every DOI was resolved through Crossref or OpenAlex. Numbers come from the abstract unless the row says
"full text". "Fits" means fits AppTime's rule that it **never blocks**.

## Interventions with a measured effect

| # | Intervention | Study | Design | Measured effect | Strength | Fits "never blocks"? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Delay + reflection prompt before opening an app, with a "don't open" button (One Sec) | Grüning, Riedel, Lorenz-Spreen 2023, *PNAS*, [10.1073/pnas.2213114120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2213114120) | Field experiment n=280, 6 wk, no control group (compared with week 1); plus a preregistered online experiment n=500 | In 36% of attempts the app was closed after the prompt; 37% fewer attempts by week 6; 57% fewer actual opens. Of the three features, the **option to dismiss** had the strongest effect, more than the wait | moderate (big n, no randomized control in the field study, co-authored by the app's maker) | **partly**: the app still opens, but only after a wait. A delay the user can't skip is friction, not a block. What worked best was offering the choice, not the wait |
| 2 | Bundle of 10 self-chosen setting changes (grayscale, notifications off, apps moved off the home screen, ...) | Olson, Sandra, Chmoulevitch, Raz, Veissière 2022, *Int J Ment Health Addict*, [10.1007/s11469-022-00826-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11469-022-00826-w) | Study 2: RCT n=70 against a "monitoring only" control, 2 wk, 6-wk follow-up n=44; full text read | About 57 min/day less screen time (control about 11 less). Problematic-use score SAS-SV (Smartphone Addiction Scale, Short Version) −5.5 vs −0.6. No difference in depression | moderate (preregistered, small, one university) | **yes**: every step is a settings change. Note: "monitoring only" barely moved anything |
| 3 | Grayscale screen | Holte & Ferraro 2020, *Social Science Journal*, [10.1080/03623319.2020.1737461](https://doi.org/10.1080/03623319.2020.1737461) | n=161, 73 in grayscale for 8–10 days, students; quasi-experimental | Total screen time −37.9 min/day against their own Time 1; drop in social media and browsing, none in video; no effect on anxiety or depression | weak–moderate | **yes**: Android has it built in (Bedtime mode / Digital Wellbeing); AppTime could suggest it |
| 4 | Batching notifications (3×/day) | Fitz, Kushlev et al. 2019, *Computers in Human Behavior*, [10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016) | Randomized field experiment n=237: usual / batched / none | Batched: more attention, more sense of control, better well-being, less stress. **No notifications at all** raised anxiety and FOMO (fear of missing out). Effect sizes not read (abstract unavailable; PDF unreadable) | moderate (direction only, see note) | **yes**: it delays, never suppresses |
| 5 | Alerts off + phone out of sight, for one week | Kushlev, Proulx, Dunn 2016, *CHI*, [10.1145/2858036.2858359](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858359) | Within-subject, n=221, 1 wk each condition | Less self-reported inattention and hyperactivity with alerts off; inattention predicted lower productivity and well-being | moderate | **yes** |
| 6 | Gentle repeated vibration after a self-set daily limit | Okeke, Sobolev, Dell, Estrin 2018, *MobileHCI*, [10.1145/3229434.3229463](https://doi.org/10.1145/3229434.3229463) | Controlled experiment n=50 (MTurk), 3 wk | Over 20% less daily use; **the effect did not last** after the vibration was removed; users reported more awareness | weak–moderate | **yes**: aversive but never blocks |
| 7 | Self-set goals + awareness app (MyTime) | Hiniker, Hong, Kohno, Kientz 2016, *CHI*, [10.1145/2858036.2858403](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858403) | Deployment n=23, 2 wk, no control | −21% time on apps the user rated "a poor use of time"; "good" apps unchanged | weak | **yes**: the user labels which apps are good or bad |
| 8 | Changing the Facebook interface: goal reminder, or newsfeed removed | Lyngs, Lukoff, Slovak, Seymour et al. 2020, *CHI*, [10.1145/3313831.3376672](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376672) | n=58 students, 3 arms, 6 wk (ABA: weeks off, on, off) | Both helped participants stay on task; reminders were annoying; removing the feed caused FOMO. No numbers in the abstract | weak | reminders **yes**; feed removal **no** (it removes content) |
| 9 | Rotating interventions (HabitLab) | Kovacs, Wu, Bernstein 2018, *CSCW*, [10.1145/3274364](https://doi.org/10.1145/3274364) | 3 in-the-wild field experiments, browser | Rotation beat a fixed intervention on time-on-site, but more users uninstalled; explaining the rotation at the moment it happened halved the drop-outs | moderate (field, large) | **partly**: some of its interventions are blockers. Lesson: a static intervention wears off |
| 10 | Pop-up alerts about excessive use, built from the user's own usage | Loid, Täht, Rozgonjuk 2020, *Computers in Human Behavior* 102:22–30, [dblp record](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/chb/LoidTR20.html) (DOI not resolved) | RCT n=73, 3 arms, 2 months | **Null**: no change in screen time, phone checks, or problematic use | moderate null | **yes**, and still failed: personal usage feedback alone did not move behaviour |
| 11 | Meta-analysis of digital self-control tools (DSCTs) | Monge Roffarello & De Russis 2023, *ACM TOCHI*, [10.1145/3571810](https://doi.org/10.1145/3571810) | Systematic review + meta-analysis | Pooled number not read (full text blocked). The authors' summary ([elite.polito.it](https://elite.polito.it/news/2023/05/31/tochi-dscts)): a significant effect in 7 of the papers analysed; "not effective in the long term"; studies often last 21 days, rarely have a control group, and mostly sample students | review (strong on gaps) | n/a: most tools reviewed block or remove |
| 12 | Map of 367 self-control tools | Lyngs et al. 2019, *CHI*, [10.1145/3290605.3300361](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300361) | Review of the app stores, no effect measured | Classifies tools with a dual-systems model; points out mechanisms tools rarely use (e.g. goal memory) | taxonomy | n/a |
| 13 | Review of "digital detox" | Radtke et al. 2021, *Mobile Media & Communication*, [10.1177/20501579211028647](https://doi.org/10.1177/20501579211028647) | Systematic review | Not read beyond the title: no claim made | — | — |

**Patterns tab (A→B→A flows, triggers):** no study found that measures feedback on **flows or triggers**
specifically. Rows 7 and 10 come closest. Showing a pattern is therefore unproven; pairing it with a
concrete next step (rows 1–4) is what has evidence. The "Olson et al. 2023 meta-analysis" does not
exist under that name: the Olson paper is the 2022 RCT in row 2.

## Insights claims audit

| Claim in the app | Source checked | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Phone presence lowers cognitive capacity" (`insights_attention_productivity.dart`, Ward et al. 2017) | Ward, Duke, Gneezy, Bos 2017, *JACR*, [10.1086/691462](https://doi.org/10.1086/691462). Meta-analyses: Hartanto et al. 2024, *Technology, Mind, and Behavior*, [10.1037/tmb0000123](https://doi.org/10.1037/tmb0000123) (full text read): 33 studies, 166 effects, **d = −0.02, 95% CI [−0.06, 0.01], p = .246**; only the OSPAN task (operation span, a working-memory test) shows d = −0.13. Parry 2024, *Media Psychology*, [10.1080/15213269.2023.2286647](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2023.2286647): k = 56, n = 7093; only working memory is significant, effect "smaller than theorized", low power | **failed replication: cut.** At most: "evidence is mixed; a small effect on working memory at best" |
| "Multitasking drops IQ by 10 points, more than a lost night's sleep" (`insight_alertas_1.dart`, cites APA) | Comes from a 2005 HP-sponsored "infomania" study by Glenn Wilson, never peer-reviewed. Coverage: [Discover](https://www.discovermagazine.com/technology/does-e-mail-make-you-dumber); critique: [generallythinking.com](https://generallythinking.com/2010/04/23/tim-ferriss-email-and-science-reporting/). The cited [APA page](https://www.apa.org/research/action/multitask) is about switch costs ("up to 40% of productive time", Meyer). A search summary says it has no IQ claim; I could not read the page directly (bot wall) | **cut**: unsupported, and wrongly attributed to the APA |
| "23 min (23 min 15 s) to refocus after an interruption" (`insight_alertas_1.dart`, `insights_attention_productivity.dart`, cites Mark 2008) | The figure is not in Mark, Gudith, Klocke 2008 [10.1145/1357054.1357072](https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357072), which found people *finish faster* when interrupted, at the cost of stress. It comes from Mark's [Gallup interview](https://news.gallup.com/businessjournal/23146/too-many-interruptions-work.aspx), about Mark, González, Harris 2005 [10.1145/1054972.1055017](https://doi.org/10.1145/1054972.1055017) (n=24 observed workers): interrupted work was **resumed** after 23 min 15 s on average, with other tasks in between. It is not "deep focus lost per interruption" | **cut or rewrite**: "× 23 min per unlock" is a misuse. If kept: "interrupted work waits ~23 min on average before it's picked up again (n=24 office workers, 2005)" |
| "21 days is the scientific benchmark" for a habit (`app_localizations_*.dart` l.201–208) | Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts, Wardle 2010, *EJSP*, [10.1002/ejsp.674](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674): n=96, 84 days; time to reach 95% of one's automaticity plateau **ranged 18–254 days**. Median 66 per [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit) (secondary). 21 days has no empirical source | **cut**: no "21 days" milestone. "Weeks to months, it varies widely; missing one day doesn't break it" is supported |
| One Sec card: "a 10-s delay dissipates most impulses" (`insight_solucoes_1.dart`) | The DOI in the app, `10.1073/pnas.2213580120`, **returns 404**; the correct one is [10.1073/pnas.2213114120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2213114120). The paper says opens fell 57% over 6 weeks, and the **dismiss option**, not the wait, did the most | **cut the card** (it advertises a third-party app). If AppTime keeps the idea: cite row 1 correctly and say "an exit option before opening" |

Other cards still link to `scholar.google.com/scholar?q=...` searches instead of DOIs (Ward 2017, Mark 2008,
Duke & Ward 2019, Meier & Reinecke 2021). Not audited here.
